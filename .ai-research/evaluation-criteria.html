Evaluation Criteria for Benchmark Tooling

Framework for assessing benchmark tools. Used to guide the design of the beer-game benchmark tool for local models.

1. Validity — Does it measure what it claims?

  • Construct validity: Does the task actually test the intended capability (reasoning vs. recall vs. memorization)?
  • Contamination resistance: Are test items absent from training data? Refreshable? Held-out?
  • Discriminative power: Can it distinguish strong from weak models, or do results cluster?
  • Ecological validity: Does performance correlate with real-world usefulness?

2. Reliability — Is it reproducible?

  • Determinism: Same input + seed → same score (temperature, sampling, tool-call ordering controlled).
  • Test-retest stability: Re-running yields the same ranking and similar scores.
  • Inter-rater agreement: For LLM-as-judge or human eval, do judges agree?
  • Variance reporting: Confidence intervals and standard error, not just point estimates.

3. Coverage & Difficulty

  • Breadth: Range of skills/domains/scenarios covered.
  • Depth: Does it stress frontier models, or is it saturated?
  • Difficulty calibration: Distribution of easy/medium/hard items.
  • Headroom: Room for improvement remaining at the top.

4. Fairness & Robustness

  • Prompt sensitivity: Small wording changes shouldn't flip results.
  • Format bias: Avoid privileging certain output styles (MCQ vs. free-form).
  • Tokenizer fairness: No undue disadvantage to specific languages or model families.
  • Bias/safety dimensions: Report demographic and content disparities where relevant.

5. Tooling Quality (Engineering)

  • Ease of integration: Minutes to plug in a new model.
  • Model interface flexibility: OpenAI API, HF Transformers, vLLM, Ollama, llama.cpp, MLX.
  • Sandboxing & safety: Safe execution of untrusted code/agent actions (Docker, gVisor, Firejail).
  • Parallelism & speed: Async, batching, request pipelining.
  • Caching & resumability: Skip completed items; resume after crashes.
  • Logging & traces: Full request/response logs for debugging.
  • Extensibility: Low friction for new tasks, solvers, and scorers.

6. Cost & Efficiency

  • Total tokens & wall-clock time per full run.
  • Compute requirements: Local vs. API; GPU/CPU/RAM footprint.
  • Cost per eval: Especially important for closed-API models.
  • Sample efficiency: Can a small subset (~200 items) approximate the full run?

7. Reporting & Interpretability

  • Per-task breakdown, not just aggregate scores.
  • Statistical significance testing between models.
  • Error analysis support: Inspect why a model failed.
  • Comparability: Standardized metrics across models and runs.
  • Leaderboard transparency: Methodology and code public; results reproducible.

8. Maintenance & Governance

  • Active development: Recent commits, issue responsiveness, release cadence.
  • Versioning: Task/dataset versions pinned for cross-time reproducibility.
  • Community & adoption: Citations, dependent projects, leaderboard usage.
  • Documentation quality: Quickstart, API reference, examples.
  • License: Permissive (Apache/MIT) preferred for downstream use.

9. Agent-Specific (Beer-Game Relevant)

  • Multi-turn support: Persistent state across turns.
  • Tool-use protocol: Function calling, MCP, custom action spaces.
  • Environment fidelity: Realistic state, partial observability, delays.
  • Trajectory scoring: Evaluate reasoning quality and decision sequences, not just final answer.
  • Replayability: Deterministic environments for re-running and debugging.

Application to the Beer-Game Tool

Priority axes given the use case (benchmarking local models on strategic, multi-turn decision-making):

  1. Agent support — multi-turn, stateful, partial observability are core to the beer game.
  2. Tooling quality — easy integration with Ollama, llama.cpp, vLLM, OpenAI-compatible endpoints.
  3. Cost & speed — local inference is slow; batching, caching, and resumability matter.
  4. Reliability — deterministic demand patterns, fixed seeds, reproducible runs.
  5. Validity — vary demand scenarios to resist memorization; measure bullwhip amplification, total cost, cost variance.
  6. Reporting — per-position breakdown (retailer/wholesaler/distributor/factory), trajectory traces, cost decomposition.
